# A7.41 — position readability audit

**READ-ONLY DIAGNOSTIC. No database mutation, no scoring change, no position-mapping change.**
Production scoring unchanged. Artifact: `A7.41-position-audit.json`, regenerated by
`server/scripts/a741PositionAudit.js`.

# VERDICT: **MIXED — a data-quality gap, a latent evidence gap, and a contamination finding**

The 1,756 rows are **not 1,756 problems**. They are one recorded sentinel, produced by three
different upstream causes, of which **72 rows are not a position problem at all — they are the wrong
squad**. Prevalence is 2.8% of the roster; the ranking consequence is **0.5–0.9% of cells**.

---

## 1–2. Baseline, and what "unreadable" actually means

| | men's | women's |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 roster rows | 29,000 | 33,257 |
| readable position | 28,113 | 32,388 |
| **A — raw position absent** | **0** | **0** |
| **B — present but parser rejects** | **887** | **869** |
| C–H (taxonomy gap, ambiguity, malformed…) | **0** | **0** |

**Category A is empty and so is everything after B.** Every unreadable row carries a value; the
parser rejects it; nothing else contributes. `readPosition` reads `canonicalPosition` through a null
check, so a rejected value becomes doubt rather than an `'UNKNOWN'` bucket — that guard works.

## 3. Raw-value taxonomy — **one value**

| raw value | men | women | programmes | divisions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`"UNKNOWN"`** | **887** | **869** | **310** | D1, D2, D3, NAIA, USCAA |

**There is no second value.** The stored position is a literal sentinel — a *recorded absence*
written upstream, the exact mirror of A7.39 where every label parsed and 752 were simply missing.

## 4. Pipeline trace — the loss is at acquisition, and is already recorded there

The 2026 acquisition CSVs contain **1,756 blank Position cells**, reconciling exactly with the
database. `importRosterSheets.js` maps blank → `'UNKNOWN'` via `normalizePosition` and stores it
faithfully.

**The CSVs carry only five distinct values**: `Midfielder` 20,232 · `Defender` 19,078 · `Forward`
14,564 · `Goalkeeper` 6,627 · blank 1,756. So the pipeline's `lib.position()` collapses every source
label to the four groups **before Thriv3 sees anything**, and returns `''` when its `POSMAP` matches
nothing.

**Root cause: `ACQUISITION_MISSED_FIELD` and `SOURCE_MISSING`, in a proportion that varies by
programme.** Not `PARSER_DEFECT`, not `MATCHMAKING_TAXONOMY_GAP`.

## 5. Source validation — three distinct causes, and one of them is not about position

Re-ran the pipeline's own extractor against five guard-flagged programmes:

| programme | mode | parsed | raw positions | mapped | what the cell actually held |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saint Francis (PA) | sidearm-html | 16 | 16 | **16** | Midfielder, Forward, Defender, Goalkeeper |
| Thomas Jefferson | sidearm-html | 35 | 35 | **35** | …incl. **`Defender/Midfielder`** |
| **Grand Canyon** (W) | nuxt | 48 | 20 | **0** | **`DIS`, `Distance`** — track & field |
| **Kansas State** (W) | nuxt | 35 | 30 | **0** | **`Distance`, `Jumps`, `Throws`, `MD`** |
| Union (KY) | table | 44 | 0 | 0 | no position column captured |

1. **Recoverable** — the page exposes positions and the pipeline maps 100% of them today. Same shape
   as A7.40: the field exists and the acquisition missed it.
2. **CONTAMINATION** — see §8.
3. **Genuinely missing** — no position column reached the extractor.

## 6. Position ontology — no gap

Matchmaking needs exactly `['GOALKEEPER', 'DEFENSE', 'MIDFIELD', 'FORWARD']`, and exactly those four
arrive. `shared/positions.js` carries a much richer vocabulary — `CB`, `RB`, `WB`, `CDM`, `CAM`,
`RW`, `ST` — which is **dead for stored rows**, because the acquisition already collapsed them. It
is pinned by test anyway: it is the contract for any future source that sends real labels.

**No `TAXONOMY_CHANGE_REQUIRED`.** Nothing is being rejected for want of a category.

## 7. Multi-position — collapsed at acquisition, and lost twice over

**Zero multi-position labels exist in the database.** They are collapsed before storage: the pipeline
takes the token before the first separator, and `canonicalPosition` does the same again.

Thomas Jefferson's live page publishes **`Defender/Midfielder`**. A player listed there is counted as
competition at DEFENSE and **as nobody at MIDFIELD**. The information is gone at the type level — the
function returns a bare string, so a caller cannot discover that a second group existed.

**Recorded as a defect, not repaired.** The fix is a *representation* question — whether the roster
index can hold a player in two groups — not a parsing one.

## 8. Acquisition regression — and a contamination finding

The A7.40 guard, **unmodified**, applied to `position`:

| signal | programmes | rows |
|---|---|---|
| **CATASTROPHIC_FIELD_LOSS** | **14** | ~508 |
| **FIELD_COVERAGE_DROP** | **11** | ~72 |
| OK (consistent with own history) | 294 | ~1,176 |
| NO_BASELINE | 9 | — |

Then a second discriminator, because a programme that "lost its position field" may instead have been
given a different roster. A genuine new squad repeats a substantial share of last season's names:

| programme | 2026 | 2025 | name overlap | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grand Canyon** (W) | 43 | 41 | **0%** | **DIFFERENT SQUAD** |
| **Kansas State** (W) | 29 | 29 | **0%** | **DIFFERENT SQUAD** |
| Saint Francis (PA) | 15 | 28 | 4% | suspect |
| Union (KY) … George Mason | — | — | 32–74% | plausible continuation |

**Grand Canyon and Kansas State women's soccer hold rows that are not women's soccer players.** The
stored names include *Michael Archie*, *Angelo Gordon*, *Brady Grunder*, *Nick Albus*; the positions
the extractor read were `DIS`, `Jumps`, `Throws`. The `nuxt` extractor landed on a different sport's
roster — evidently men's track and field — and 72 rows were filed as women's soccer.

> **The unreadable position is not the defect here. It is the symptom that saved us.**
> `lib.position()` correctly refused to map a track event, which is the only reason these rows were
> visible at all. Had that page carried plausible soccer positions, the contamination would have
> shipped silently — and A7.40's turnover gate would not have caught it either, because a wholly
> different squad reads as 100% turnover, which looks like a *healthy* new roster.

## 9. Matchmaking dependency trace — the gap is a dead field

`buildPositionIndex` increments a **programme-level** `unreadable` for a row whose position it cannot
read, and its own comment says why that matters:

> *"an unplaceable player is a gap in what we know about every position"*

**That counter reaches diagnostic scripts and report prose, and no scorer.** Both
`returningCompetition` and `positionalOpportunity` are handed the **bucket-level** `unreadable` — and
a row with no readable position is **in no bucket**. So it is invisible to the very guard A7.37 added.

| | men's | women's |
|---|---|---|
| programmes carrying unreadable-position rows | 192 | 159 |
| **programme-position cells in those programmes** | **718 of 3,472 = 20.7%** | **570 of 4,647 = 12.3%** |

## 10. False absence — **confirmed, and it is A7.36's asymmetry one layer up**

A programme with 8 forwards, 3 readable and 5 `UNKNOWN`, produces a bucket of `rows: 3`,
`unreadable: 0` — because `bucket.unreadable` counts unreadable **class**, not unreadable
**position**. A7.37's `readableShare` therefore reads **1.0: fully readable**. If those 3 depart,
the cell scores `competition = 1.000`, **grade MEASURED, coverage 1**, with five players of unknown
position unaccounted.

At entry 2027:

| | men's | women's |
|---|---|---|
| MEASURED zero-returner cells | 52 | 136 |
| **of those, in a programme holding position-unreadable rows** | **19 (36.5%)** | **40 (29.4%)** |
| unaccounted players behind them | **219** | **290** |

**UNKNOWN does become positive evidence, for roughly a third of the confident-zero cells.**
Not repaired, by instruction.

## 11. Current-horizon scale — prevalence is not consequence

| | prevalence | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| rows | 1,756 = **2.8%** of the roster | — |
| cells in affected programmes | **20.7% / 12.3%** | — |
| **cells scoring a possibly-false MEASURED zero** | — | **19 / 4,636 and 40 / 4,636 = 0.5% / 0.9%** |

The ranking exposure is **two orders of magnitude smaller than the row count**, and smaller than the
completeness bias A7.40 closed. It is real, bounded, and not urgent.

## 14. Guard coverage

**Caught:** 25 programmes / 580 rows, with **no modification** — the A7.40 guard is genuinely
field-agnostic, as designed.
**Not caught:** the 1,176 chronic rows, correctly — flagging 294 programmes whose coverage matches
their own history would bury the 25 that matter.
**False positives:** none observed. The 14 catastrophic flags are all real field losses; two of them
turned out to be something worse.
**No guard change recommended.** The discriminator that found the contamination is a *second* check
and belongs beside the guard, not inside it.

## 17–18. Recommended A7.42

**A7.42 — the 72 contaminated rows, first and on its own.** It is small, it is a correctness defect
rather than a coverage one, and it is the only finding here where the data is *wrong* rather than
*absent*. Confirm both rosters at source, then remove or replace them; check whether any other
programme was acquired by the `nuxt` extractor onto a different sport's roster — the cheap test is
zero name overlap with the prior season, which needs no network access.

**Then, separately:** declare the programme-level `unreadable` to the scorers, so §10's false absence
is closed the way A7.37 closed its own. That is a *scoring-evidence* change and needs its own
preregistration — it is exactly the kind of change A7.37 showed can shift a distribution.

**Not recommended:** re-acquiring the 508 recoverable rows before the extractor is fixed upstream, for
the reason A7.40 recorded — the fix lives in `~/Documents/Thriv3/_roster_pipeline/lib.py`, and
repairing data ahead of it just re-runs the same loss next season.
