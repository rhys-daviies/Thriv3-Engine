## A. The corpus, and why it is stable while 7B is writing

V2 and Phase 7B share one SQLite file. That is a fact about the workspace, not
a defect, and it means the A8 baseline cannot be validated against "the
database" — it is validated against a corpus whose identity is stated.

**What V2 reads.** Four production query sites, all in
`server/lib/v2/poolContext.js`, over exactly three tables: `colleges`,
`roster_players`, `recruiting_arrivals`. `shared/matching/v2/` contains no
database access at all — it is pure functions over a context the caller builds.

**What 7B writes.** `refresh/promotion.js` can write `coaches`,
`roster_players`, `colleges`, `athletics_domains`,
`programme_membership_periods` and the `refresh_*` ledger.

**Where they overlap.** `roster_players` and `colleges`. `coaches` is *not* a
V2 input — V2's "Coach Recruitability" is a layer name, and `recruitingRules.js`
states in terms that no coach reply, offer or outreach response is held. So
coach data is separated by construction, and the overlap is real but narrow.

**What is actually staged.** Two `STAGED` batches, zero promotions ever
applied. 5,004 `INSERT_ROSTER_ROW` and 3 `CREATE_COACH`. Every one of the 5,004
resolves to an NJCAA or USCAA programme; none touches a four-year programme.

**Proof, not assumption.** The staged batch was replayed onto a *copy* of the
corpus and the full baseline rebuilt from both. Result:

| universe | cells | differing | rank moves | value moves | state moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | 9,794 | **0** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| UNSUPPORTED | 2,690 | 1,042 | **0** | **0** | **0** |

The SUPPORTED-universe digest is byte-identical across the two corpora. The
1,042 unsupported differences are *refusal reasons becoming more precise* —
`NO_ROSTER_ON_FILE` → `NO_CLASS_LABELS` / `NO_READABLE_POSITIONS` — because
those programmes now have a roster and still cannot be ranked. Not one of them
gains a rank, a value or a state.

So: **the baseline is stable against what 7B has staged.** This claim is
bounded to what is staged. It is not a prediction about future 7B writes, and
two of them would invalidate the baseline — a roster write touching a four-year
programme, and any `colleges` write (`active`, `division`, `conference`), since
deactivating or reclassifying a programme changes the pool itself.

Rather than remember that caution, `server/scripts/a8CorpusGuard.js` measures
it. The digest is taken **twice**, over the two universes separately, because a
single whole-corpus digest answers the wrong question: it moves whenever 7B
lands two-year data, and a digest that cries wolf is one people learn to
override. SUPPORTED moving means the baseline is stale. UNSUPPORTED moving is
expected and healthy.

## B. The validation universe

`FOUR_YEAR_SUPPORTED_UNIVERSE` is **derived, not listed**. A division is
supported exactly when `shared/eligibility.js` holds a rule for it, so
researching a new association moves it into the supported universe with no
second edit. A hand-written list would be a second copy of a fact that file
already owns, and the two would disagree the first time somebody updated one.

| | men's | women's | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUPPORTED — NCAA D1/D2/D3, NAIA | 917 | 1,229 | **2,146** |
| UNSUPPORTED — NJCAA, USCAA, CCCAA, NCCAA, NWAC | 288 | 193 | **481** |
| pool | 1,205 | 1,422 | 2,627 |

**The engine already separates them, but by accident rather than by rule.**
There is no universe filter anywhere in the scoring path; `UNRULED_DIVISIONS`
exists only to choose a note string. All 481 unsupported programmes land in
`LIMITED_DATA` because without an eligibility rule the evidence refuses — and,
as §A shows, they stay there even once they have full rosters. That is the
right outcome reached by the right mechanism, but an *emergent* property can
break silently, so structural test **S6** pins it.

Confirmed for the pipeline: every eligible programme is evaluated before
ranking; the Top 100 is a prefix of the full ordering, not a separate model
(**S11**); #101+ are retained, not discarded; and `LIMITED_DATA` programmes
keep a named refusal rather than becoming a low score (**S14**).

### Gaps found — reported, not closed

The brief forbids altering athlete profiles and requires new athletes to be
justified first. These are therefore reported for decision:

1. **`intended_major` is `null` for all ten.** `majorFit` is a real Opportunity
   component with weight 1.2 and a binary ±0.40/−0.60 deviation, and it returns
   `notApplicable` for every one of the 12,484 cells. **Acceptance category 5
   cannot be assessed for major fit with the current set.** It is closable:
   2,298 of 2,627 programmes (87.5%) carry `notable_majors`, so a declared
   major would genuinely exercise the component rather than produce refusals.
2. **Every athlete enters in 2028.** The entry year is two seasons past the
   2026 roster, so the A7.37 horizon depth is exercised at exactly one distance.
   Nothing tests depth 1 or 3.
3. **No athlete leaves the budget unstated.** A7.48B proved the `Undeclared` /
   `NEEDS_CONFIRMATION` representation path at the unit level, but no reference
   athlete exercises it end to end.
4. `academic_minimum` is `null` throughout; no athlete is a redshirt.

Gap 1 is the one that blocks a preregistered acceptance category. Gaps 2–4
narrow coverage without blocking anything.

## D. Acceptance categories

Assessed independently. **Not** collapsed into one number — a single "accuracy"
figure would let a hard-constraint violation be averaged away by good ranking.

1. **Hard-constraint correctness.** No ranking quality compensates for a
   violation.
2. **Evidence truthfulness.** UNKNOWN ≠ ZERO. UNSCOREABLE ≠ BAD. PARTIAL ≠
   MEASURED.
3. **Recruitability plausibility.** Credible without turning prestige into
   recruitability.
4. **Financial semantics.** Responds to the stated contribution without
   assuming aid.
5. **Athlete opportunity / fit.** Playing Pathway, programme trajectory and
   major fit against their frozen contracts. *Major fit is currently
   unassessable — see §C gap 1.*
6. **Pursuit ordering.** A useful relative strategy across the whole supported
   universe.

## E. Structural tests — any failure is a blocker

Sixteen, in `server/scripts/a8Baseline.test.js`, run against the committed
artifact. They run against the artifact rather than the database because the
suite deliberately runs with `RECRUITMATCH_DB=':memory:'`, so a corpus-level
test would silently assert nothing.

| | asserts |
|---|---|
| S0 | the recorded digest is the digest of the cells carried |
| S1 | full-universe completeness: every athlete sees its whole pool |
| S2 | no duplicate programme within an athlete |
| S3 | every cell carries a programme id, name and division |
| S4 | ranks are a dense 1..n permutation — ties cannot collide or gap |
| S5 | rank order agrees with pursuit order |
| S6 | no UNSUPPORTED programme is ranked or scored |
| S7 | every score within [0,1] |
| S8 | no NaN or Infinity anywhere |
| S9 | a layer is scoreable with a value or unscoreable with a reason, never both or neither |
| S10 | RANKED requires all three of R/F/O |
| S11 | the Top 100 is the first 100 of the full ranking |
| S12 | the engine's account of what is missing matches which layers refused |
| S13 | every grade is MEASURED or PARTIAL — ASSUMED must not return |
| S14 | LIMITED_DATA stays visible and never becomes a low score |
| S15 | no person-level field reached the artifact |

**These were shown to fail.** Twelve deliberately broken artifacts were built,
one per failure mode, and each was caught by its own test —
`A8_BASELINE_FILE` exists for exactly that purpose. A preregistered blocker
test that has never been seen to fail is an assertion about itself.

## F. Preference responsiveness

Vary **one** preference at a time on controlled copies of the same athlete,
across the eight `PROFILES`.

Success is **semantic, not positional**. It is *not* "these schools must rise".
It is: changing a preference changes authority **only through the mechanism
that preference controls**.

Measured per variation: rank displacement distribution; Top 25/50/100 churn;
direction of the underlying attribute the preference names; numeric change in
layers the preference does not control (expected: none); and coupling effects
between preferences.

A preference that moves a layer it has no authority over is a defect regardless
of whether the resulting ranking looks better. No hidden preference may
dominate.

## G. Evidence ablation

Remove one evidence source at a time from controlled cells: readable positions
→ unreadable; class evidence → unavailable; rotation history → unavailable;
major evidence → unavailable; financial evidence → unavailable where valid;
recruiting-market evidence → unavailable.

Required behaviour in every case:

> loss of knowledge → reduced evidence / PARTIAL / UNSCOREABLE per the frozen
> contract

Forbidden in every case:

> loss of knowledge → an invented negative score

This tests the architecture globally. It does not reopen component design.

Note a property established in A7.45 that governs how these results must be
read: `coverage.combine()` derives layer coverage from **weights**, never from
component coverage, so **a component's own coverage never restrains its
contribution**. Only refusals move rankings. An ablation that lowers coverage
but changes no ranking is therefore the *expected* result, not a null finding.

## I. Outlier selection

Preregistered and rule-based, in `server/scripts/a8Outliers.js`, so the review
set cannot become "the examples that make V2 look right". Every criterion
selects cases that are **awkward** for the model, and the disagreement rules
are deliberately symmetric so neither direction can be quietly dropped.

Layer comparisons are computed on **within-fixture percentile ranks**, never on
raw values: the layers are not on a common scale, so comparing a raw 0.7
Recruitability with a raw 0.7 Opportunity is the same category error as
comparing a temperature with a pressure.

Criteria: `TOP_PURSUIT`; `HIGH_R_LOW_O`; `LOW_R_HIGH_O`; `HIGH_F_LOW_R`;
`WIDEST_LAYER_DISAGREEMENT`; `BOUNDARY_{25,50,100,250}` either side;
`EVIDENCE_POOR_NEAR_TOP` (weakest total coverage that still reached the first
100 — selecting on PARTIAL alone would select almost everything).

Yield: **337 cells across the 10 athletes**, ~34 each — bounded and reviewable.

## J. Review format

For each selected programme, a compact evidence card answering: why is it this
high or low; what does Thriv3 actually know; what is uncertain; which layer
drives Pursuit; which athlete preference materially affects it; and would the
explanation shown to a consultant or player tell the same story.

The harness surfaces bounded anomalies and representative examples. Nobody is
asked to classify hundreds of schools by hand.

## K. V1 comparison

V1 is frozen at `711af51`. It is a **comparison instrument, not ground truth.**

Reported: rank correlation; Top 25/50/100 overlap; major disagreement
programmes; and why each disagreement occurs.

**V2 success is not defined as reproducing V1.** V2 deliberately answers a
different, more evidence-aware question, and A7.48B measured the two as
agreeing only moderately by construction (Kendall 0.545 on the financial
comparison; swapping the layers moves 433 programmes by 100+ ranks). A large
divergence is a finding to explain, never a failure by itself.

## L. Historical destinations

Contextual evidence only. **Not** a correctness oracle:

> destination ranked higher = correct — **rejected**
> destination ranked lower = incorrect — **rejected**

Where a student actually enrolled reflects their offers, their family, their
timing and their luck. It is not a measurement of coach recruitability and not
a statement about optimal pursuit. Destinations are used only to surface
surprising cases worth explaining.

## M. Blocker classes

### BLOCKER
hard-constraint violation · unsupported programme ranked as supported ·
invented evidence · unknown treated as zero · non-deterministic ranking ·
Top 100 not a prefix of the full ranking · NaN or invalid score · explanation
materially contradicting engine state · a missing required input silently
receiving ranking authority.

### REVIEW_REQUIRED
systematic ranking behaviour that appears semantically wrong · one preference
dominating unexpectedly · a major layer disagreement with no explanation ·
evidence-poor programmes systematically populating top ranks · a substantial
supported-universe coverage gap caused by engineering rather than by source
limitations.

### NOT_A_BLOCKER
V2 differing substantially from V1 · a historical destination ranking lower ·
LIMITED_DATA caused by genuinely missing evidence · an unsupported two-year or
other association not being ranked · rank movement caused by a truthful
evidence refusal.

## N. The baseline artifact

`docs/validation/A8.0-baseline.json` — 12,484 cells, one per reference athlete
× evaluated programme, 3.56 MB.

Per cell: athlete and programme index, division, universe, eligibility model,
ranking state, rank, Pursuit value and grade, missing layers, and for each of
Recruitability / Financial / Opportunity its state, value, grade, coverage,
refusal reason and missing inputs.

A refusal and a score are the same shape, on purpose: a comparison that had to
branch on which one it held would be one that could silently skip the other.

**Encoding.** `f` and `p` are indices into `athletes[]` and `programmes[]`, and
null-valued keys are omitted. This is what keeps the artifact at 3.56 MB rather
than 11.4 MB. `decodeBaseline()` expands a cell to long form and the tests read
the long form; the codec is documented in `CELL_CODEC` / `LAYER_CODEC`. Omitting
`value: null` on a refusal is also the honest encoding — a null invites a
reader to treat a refusal as a zero, which is the one reading V2 exists to
prevent.

**Determinism.** Three consecutive rebuilds produced
`78db6413ead4baf82b6e86b49aebaf9cef8bd59befa8185fb7a5388f798a41f8`.

**PII.** Programme id, name, division and state are public institutional facts;
the athletes are synthetic. No roster player name, hometown or contact field is
read into the artifact, and **S15** fails if one appears.
