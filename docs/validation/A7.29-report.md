# 7. Are VERIFY_EARLY and PRE_READ genuinely distinct actions? — **Yes**

Three pieces of evidence:

1. **They separate cleanly on the easy axis and are hard on the intended one.** NORMAL versus
   not-NORMAL was unanimous on all 17; the two concern levels were genuinely hard on five or six.
   That is the signature of a real distinction rather than a manufactured one.
2. **VERIFY_EARLY is not a dumping ground.** It was assigned 4 times and was correct 4 times, with
   zero over-escalation into it and zero out of it. A middle level that absorbed uncertainty would
   show over-assignment; this one does not.
3. **The downstream behaviour differs.** Trinity at −316/26% stays in the athlete's plan while the
   question is asked; Carleton at −417/20% does not. One changes the order of a conversation, the
   other changes whether a programme is load-bearing in a recruiting plan.

# 8. Is the severity boundary directionally right? — **Yes, with a shape defect**

Monotone, never inverted, never over-escalating. The defect is that the shortfall cut is a
**constant** where the evidence says it must **fall as the institution becomes more selective**.

The frontier read off the 17 blind answers, not fitted:

| acceptance | VERIFY_EARLY observed at | PRE_READ observed at | frontier lies between |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5–10%** | *none* | 251, 283, 303, 420, 596 | **≤ 250** (not probed below) |
| **10–20%** | 248 | 280, 407 | **248–280** |
| **20–30%** | 250, 316 | 417 | **316–417** |
| **30–45%** | 277 | 347, 381 | **277–347** |

Monotone increasing in acceptance, with overlapping brackets and no contradiction. The mechanism the
blind reasoning kept returning to was not probability but **band width**: a 5% institution admits a
narrow slice and coach support moves a recruit a short distance inside it; a 40% institution admits
a wide slice and the same shortfall is already inside.

# 9. The form of a future boundary — **not fitted here**

**Proposed form: a small number of interpretable acceptance bands, each with its own shortfall cut.**
Three or four bands, matching the table above.

| candidate form | verdict |
|---|---|
| **stepped bands** | **preferred** — explainable to an operator in one sentence, robust to boundary position (A7.27 measured the identified set as stable across a 150-point shortfall range), and it is what the evidence actually shows |
| monotone curve, e.g. cut = −(a + b·acceptance) | fits two coefficients to 17 cards, which is exactly what A7.22 proved worthless; and it is harder to explain in a product surface |
| single joint index | opaque, and would reintroduce the "unknown becomes a number" failure the evidence states exist to prevent |

**No coefficients are proposed and the current boundary is not altered.**

**The change is small and bounded**, which is the reassuring part. Re-levelling moves programmes
**between** the two levels and adds or removes nothing: the union stays at **10.1 per top-100**
across all 56 runs. The population that could move is `ACADEMIC_REVIEW` at acceptance under 12% —
**84 across 56 runs, 1.5 per top-100**, with a median shortfall of only −153, so most of them would
not move under any plausible cut.

# 10. GPA — context only, for now

**How it is used today: it is not.** The shadow reads athlete SAT, programme SAT and acceptance
rate. GPA appears on the card and in no calculation.

**Should it contribute to severity? Not yet, and the reason is a data gap, not a judgement.**
There is **no programme-side GPA evidence anywhere in the database** — no average GPA, no
minimum, no range, for any of 2,406 programmes. A GPA can therefore only ever be read against a
general sense of what is weak, never against *this institution*, which is precisely the comparison
that makes the SAT evidence usable.

**It did change one answer, and that is worth recording rather than generalising.** On the Millsaps
card a 2.2 GPA with an 800 SAT was weak in **absolute** terms rather than merely relative, and that
pushed a 43%-acceptance card to the stronger action when the shortfall alone would not have. On
every other card GPA moved in lockstep with SAT and added nothing.

**Two honest caveats.** The ladder pairs GPA and SAT by construction, so **this pack cannot separate
their contributions** — any apparent GPA effect is confounded. And this is exactly the risk the
brief names: letting SAT become synonymous with academic viability. It already has, in the shadow,
by default rather than by decision.

**Verdict: context only, displayed but not scored, pending programme-side GPA evidence.** An
absolute-weakness floor is the one plausible GPA role the evidence hints at, and one card is not
grounds for building it.

# 11. Test policy — unknown everywhere, and it must stay unknown

Confirmed by schema inspection: **no `test_policy` field exists on any table.** The three
policy-named columns in the database (`outreach_send.policy_version`,
`programme_messages.policy_version`, `programme_messages.sequence_policy_version`) are messaging
versions and unrelated.

So for this phase: `UNKNOWN` for all 2,406 programmes, stated on every card as "not on file", and
**nothing was inferred from a missing SAT average**. A missing average is a missing average; it is
not evidence of a test-blind policy.

How future evidence would change the action:

| policy | effect |
|---|---|
| `REQUIRED` | the published average is the enrolled body's; the shortfall reads at full weight, as today |
| `OPTIONAL` | the average reflects **submitters only** and is biased upward, so the shortfall is overstated — the same shortfall should sit one level lower, and the athlete may simply not submit |
| `BLIND` | the institution does not read the score; the comparison is **dropped, not discounted**, evidence falls back to acceptance alone, and `PRE_READ` becomes unreachable on test evidence |
| `UNKNOWN` | today's behaviour |

**Not implemented.** No card in this pack carried a known policy, so none of this was exercised.

# 16. `test_policy` collection plan

Smallest viable ingestion. **Design only — not executed, no research performed.**

**Schema** — one row per institution, not per programme, because admissions policy is institutional:

| field | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `unitid` | integer | the existing join key; policy is institutional, so it is keyed on the institution and read by both sports' rows |
| `test_policy` | enum | `REQUIRED` · `OPTIONAL` · `BLIND` · `UNKNOWN` — four values, defaulting to `UNKNOWN` |
| `test_policy_source` | text | follows the `academic_rating_source` pattern: what was read, not who read it |
| `test_policy_source_url` | text | the page the value came from |
| `test_policy_observed_at` | date | **required whenever the value is not `UNKNOWN`** — policies change annually and a value without a date is not evidence |
| `test_policy_applies_to_cycle` | text | e.g. `2027-28`; many institutions publish policy per admissions cycle |
| `test_policy_evidence_state` | enum | `STATED` (the institution's own admissions page) · `REPORTED` (a reputable aggregator) · `INFERRED` (**never written by ingestion**; reserved so that a future inference is visibly distinguishable) · `UNKNOWN` |

**Preferred public source types, in precedence order:** the institution's own admissions page
(`STATED`); the Common Data Set section C8, which most institutions publish and which states the
policy in a fixed format (`STATED`); a reputable national aggregator (`REPORTED`). **Never** inferred
from a missing SAT average — that is the exact mistake §11 forbids.

**Refresh strategy:** annually, before the recruiting cycle turns, plus an ad-hoc refresh whenever a
value is older than two cycles. Partial coverage is useful immediately because the value is
per-institution and not an aggregate — 200 institutions covered is 200 institutions where a
shortfall can be read correctly.

**Start with the institutions where it changes an answer**: the ~1,500 programmes that publish an
SAT average and carry an acceptance rate under 50%. Everywhere else the field is interesting and not
yet load-bearing.
