# A7.27 — academic recruitability calibration

**Read-only. No scoring implemented.** Production remains `0ae0399`. `majorFit` 1.20, Candidate G,
`academicStrengthPriority` authority and `playingPathway` all untouched.

**Headline: the missing dimension is confirmed at scale, its shape is now known, and the shape is
not the obvious one.** An admissions signal keyed on the test-score gap alone would be wrong **91%
of the time** for a weak-academic athlete. Keyed on the gap *and* institutional selectivity together
it identifies a small, stable set that sits at production ranks **1, 1, 2, 2, 5 and 8**.

| c07, c08 | plausible academics | +8, +12 | 95%, 78% | 4, 13 | no concern |

| c14 | no programme evidence at all | — | — | 16 | insufficient evidence |
| c15 | athlete has no test score | — | 9% | 9 | insufficient evidence |

**The single most important number in this phase: every substantial-or-worse verdict sits at
production rank 1, 1, 2, 2, 5 or 8.** The exposure is not in the tail; it is concentrated exactly
where the athlete looks first.

**The verdicts are monotone in both quantities and in neither alone.** At acceptance under 20%:
−98 reads reasonable, −398 to −603 read extreme. At 26%: −316 reads substantial. Above 90%
acceptance: −142 and −301 read as **no concern at all**. At any acceptance rate a non-negative gap
reads as no concern.

**The three cross-cutting answers, as given blind:**

- **X1 — should evidence ever stop a pursuit outright?** No. A supported recruit is a real route and
  nothing visible shows whether a coach has a place. But there is a point where a programme should
  stop being presented as a settled recommendation and start being conditional on a pre-read — and
  reaching it **requires both a large shortfall and a selective institution**.
- **X2 — missing or partial evidence?** Say so, distinctly. And note the asymmetry: a **high**
  acceptance rate is adequate evidence that admission is not an obstacle, while a **low** one is not
  adequate evidence that it is. Partial evidence may clear a case and must never condemn one.
- **X3 — acceptance rate alone?** Almost nothing, proved in both directions by c12 and c11.

# 7. Selectivity is not viability — measured, not asserted

This is the trap the brief names, and it is the largest quantitative result of the phase.

| athlete profile | programmes with a ≥300 shortfall | of those, acceptance under 20% | **would be flagged in error** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| C good (3.5/1250) | 14 | 14 | **0%** |
| D moderate (3.2/1100) | 77 | 46 | **40%** |
| E weak (2.8/950) | 189 | 46 | **76%** |
| F very weak (2.2/800) | 406 | 49 | **88%** |
| G extreme (1.0/400) | 569 | 49 | **91%** |

A design keyed on the shortfall alone is wrong for the great majority of what it touches, and gets
worse the weaker the athlete — precisely the athlete it exists to protect. Keyed on shortfall **and**
selectivity jointly, the identified set **saturates at 46–49** and never grows, because the number
of genuinely selective institutions in any top 100 is bounded.

**And the joint rule is not knife-edge.** Top-25 counts summed over all eight base athletes:

| | acceptance <25% | <20% | <15% | <10% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shortfall ≥250 | 20 | 19 | 18 | 13 |
| ≥300 | 20 | 19 | 18 | 13 |
| ≥350 | 20 | 19 | 18 | 13 |
| ≥400 | 20 | 19 | 18 | 13 |

Identical across a 150-point shortfall range and a 25%→15% selectivity range. **The design risk of
choosing a boundary in this family is low** — the only sensitive corner is acceptance below 10%
(19 → 13), and the one profile where the shortfall cut matters is C good (17 → 8 → 0 from −250 to
−350), which is exactly where the substantial-versus-extreme judgement sits.

| evidence shape | what a design must do |
|---|---|
| athlete SAT + programme SAT (+ admit) | the only shape that can support a constraint |
| athlete SAT, programme SAT missing, admit rate present | may **clear** (c13), may never condemn |
| athlete SAT, no programme evidence at all | state unknown (c14) — no adjustment either way |

| admit rate only | high rate clears; low rate is not evidence about this athlete (c12) |
| conflicting: large shortfall, high acceptance | **must not fire** (c11) — this is the 91% error case |

# 9–11. The four architecture families

| | **A. Flag only** | **B. Soft influence** | **C. Extreme tail + flag** | **D. Combined** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **semantics** | states evidence, claims nothing | admissions competes continuously inside R | conditional recommendation in a bounded region | B's semantics plus C's |
| **unknown data** | trivially safe — "unknown" is a legal state | **fails**: 16–38% of every list needs *a number*, and any number is a silent penalty or bonus | safe — fires only on MEASURED + both conditions; everything else is exactly neutral | inherits B's failure |
| **false negatives** | **total** — the five extreme cases stay at ranks 1, 1, 2, 2, 5, 8 | low | moderate — c05 (−316 at 26%, **rank 1**) would not fire | low |
| **false positives** | none (a flag is cheap to ignore) | **high** unless keyed jointly — and keyed jointly it collapses into C | near zero by construction: 0 of the 117 trap programmes | inherits B's |
| **Candidate G** | untouched | **changes R, therefore the recruitability gate, therefore P everywhere** — the largest blast radius available | untouched **if** applied as a Pursuit-level factor; moves the gate if placed inside R | untouched at best |
| **`majorFit`** | untouched | untouched | untouched — and c13 confirms the major must not offset admission | untouched |
| **international** | safe | **discriminatory in effect**: a no-test athlete has 0 MEASURED pairs, so a gradual signal would separate athletes by evidence availability rather than by evidence | safe — factor exactly 1 throughout, no penalty for a missing field, and no protection either | as B |
| **explainability** | excellent | poor — a continuous academic number on every row invites reading it as an admissions probability | excellent — "conditional on a pre-read" is a sentence a coach would recognise | mixed |

**B is rejected on the measured evidence** — not because gradual influence is wrong in principle,
but because the data to calibrate it does not exist (§12), the unknown fraction is 16–38%, and any
keying that avoids the 91% false-positive rate collapses it into C. **D is rejected while B is.**

**A is necessary and insufficient**: it costs nothing, it is available today for 62–84 of every 100
programmes, and it changes nothing about the fact that the extreme cases occupy ranks 1 and 2.

**C is the strongest single family**, with one honest gap: it does not reach c05, a rank-1 programme
316 points above the athlete at a 26%-acceptance institution, which the blind review called
substantial.

**The recommendation is A + C** — universal flagging plus a bounded extreme-tail constraint. Note
this is *not* the brief's family D, which pairs the flag and the tail with the rejected soft
influence.

**Stated plainly: we cannot currently learn how much latitude a recruited athlete receives, and
nothing in this phase estimates it.** Every verdict in §6 rests on domain reasoning about how
institution types differ, not on data we hold.

# 13. Semantic placement — Recruitability confirmed, structure refined

The candidate semantic holds:

> *Given the athlete's academic profile and the programme's available admissions evidence, how
> plausible is it that a coach could successfully recruit this athlete through the institution's
> admissions process?*

**c10 is the proof.** Ability 9 against a programme of almost exactly the athlete's own level, cost
within budget, opportunity 0.94 — everything the model can see says pursue, and the coach still
cannot deliver the place. That is a recruitability question and nothing else: not eligibility
(nobody is ineligible), not financial (it is affordable), not opportunity (the athlete wants it).

**But it is not a scalar.** c14 and c15 are *insufficient evidence*, which is a different statement
from *middling plausibility*, and the blind review kept them apart without being prompted to.

So the structure is a **contextual modifier carrying an explicit evidence state**, in Recruitability:

- **Not a component inside `support`.** `support` is a weighted average; a fourth term forces the
  unknown 16–38% to take a number. The brief forbids this and the data agrees.
- **Not a confidence value on R.** A pure confidence changes no ordering, and §6 shows ordering is
  the problem.
- **Not a Pursuit gate in the existing sense.** The two shipped gates are tail gates on continuous
  layer values; this fires on a discrete evidence condition.

Three states, only one of which does anything: `CONSTRAINING` (MEASURED, both conditions met),
`CLEAR` (MEASURED or adequately PARTIAL, conditions not met), `UNKNOWN` (everything else, factor
exactly 1).

**One open design question, flagged not decided:** whether the modifier multiplies `R` — which moves
the recruitability gate and therefore touches Candidate G — or multiplies Pursuit after the gates,
which leaves G untouched. §9 assumes the latter; the former needs its own parity proof.

# 14. Rank, flag, constrain, or combine? — **flag universally, constrain the extreme tail, do not rank**

- **Do not rank.** 16–38% of each list is unspeakable; there is no latitude data to calibrate a
  magnitude (§12); and A7.25 established that amplifying an uncalibrated signal makes recommendations
  worse.
- **Flag, always, wherever evidence is MEASURED or adequately PARTIAL.** True, useful, already
  available for 62–84 of every 100 programmes, and it costs nothing in ordering authority.
- **Constrain only the extreme tail**, only on MEASURED evidence, only where a large shortfall and a
  selective institution co-occur — the joint condition, never either alone.
- **Never remove a programme.** X1 is explicit: a supported recruit is a real route we cannot see.

# 15. Smallest justified next experiment

**Not a scoring tournament.** The boundary study in §7 has already retired the question a tournament
would have asked — the identified set is stable at 18–20 top-25 places across a 150-point shortfall
range and a 25%→15% selectivity range, so sweeping the boundary would measure noise.

The smallest justified next step is **a flag-only shadow run**, read-only and shipping nothing:

1. Compute the three-state modifier over all twelve V3/W3 profiles at profiles C, D and E.
2. Report **only** which programmes would carry which state, and at what ranks — changing no ordering.
3. Put the resulting top-25 flags in front of one blind review, asking the single question: *is this
   flag right?* — measuring the **false-positive rate against recruitment judgement**, which is the
   one number no amount of arithmetic here can produce.

That is one harness run and one small pack. **If the flag's false-positive rate is low, the
constraint in §14 is justified and can be preregistered. If it is not, nothing should be built.**

# 16. Future operational data model — concepts only, nothing built

Two objects, matching the FACT / SIGNAL split the evidence engine already enforces.

**`programme_academic_guidance`** — a SIGNAL about a programme, accumulated over time.

| concept | why |
|---|---|
| programme + sport + season | guidance is per-programme-per-sport and goes stale |
| `test_policy` — REQUIRED / OPTIONAL / BLIND / UNKNOWN | **the single most valuable missing field.** Today a missing SAT is indistinguishable from a test-blind institution |
| `min_gpa_guidance`, `min_test_guidance` | what the coach actually says the floor is |
| `supported_recruit_band` | how far below the enrolled body a supported recruit is typically admitted — **this is the latitude number §12 says we cannot currently learn** |
| `pre_read_required`, `pre_read_window` | whether a pre-read exists at all and when |
| source, observed_at, confidence | a coach's word in March is not a fact in September |

**`athlete_programme_academic_event`** — a FACT about one athlete-programme pair, append-only.

`COACH_SAYS_ACADEMICS_SUFFICIENT` · `TRANSCRIPT_REQUESTED` · `PRE_READ_SUBMITTED` ·
`PRE_READ_POSITIVE` · `PRE_READ_CONDITIONAL` · `PRE_READ_NEGATIVE` · `ADMISSIONS_EXCEPTION_OFFERED` ·
`ADMITTED` · `DENIED` · `WAITLISTED` — each with observed_at, source and a verbatim quote where one
exists.

**The learning loop, and why it is the point.** Each event is a FACT about a pair. Aggregated across
pairs at one programme they become a SIGNAL — the observed supported band — which is exactly the
quantity §12 says is unknown and §14 says must not be invented. This is the natural home for the
signal inside Coach Recruiting Intelligence: the same place that learns which coaches reply learns
which coaches can carry an academic recruit. **Until enough events exist, the band stays UNKNOWN and
the modifier stays neutral, which is the behaviour §8 already requires.**

# 17. Remaining operator decisions

1. **How far below the enrolled body should a supported recruit be assumed to reach?** Still the
   load-bearing unknown, still not in the data, and now with a defined home (§16). Nothing can be
   preregistered until it is either decided as a product assumption or collected.
2. **Should the constraint reach c05 — a rank-1 programme 316 points above the athlete at a 26%
   acceptance rate?** The blind review called it *substantial*, not *extreme*. §7 shows this is the
   one region where the boundary genuinely matters.
3. **Does the modifier multiply R or Pursuit?** R is semantically cleaner and moves Candidate G's
   gate; Pursuit is safer and slightly less honest about where the constraint lives.
4. **Should a flagged programme still be sent outreach?** A pre-read is itself a form of outreach,
   so "flagged" may mean *contact differently* rather than *contact later*. Product decision.
5. **Collect `test_policy` now?** It is a single field, it is publicly available, and without it a
   missing `sat_avg` cannot be distinguished from a test-blind institution.
6. **The two Phase-3D follow-ups**, still open: Emmanuel (MA) and New England College men's CCC
   against women's GNAC; and the women's Little East / GNAC conference completeness backfill.
