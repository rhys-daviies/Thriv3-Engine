# A8.2 — majorFit evidence-semantics repair: preregistration

Written before the code was changed.

## §B–C What the evidence actually is

`majorFit` reads exactly two inputs: `athlete.intendedMajor` and
`college.notable_majors`. There is one programme-side source and no other.

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| provenance | College Scorecard **PCIP completion shares**, via `extract_notable_majors.py` → `importNotableMajors.js`, joined on `unitid` |
| is the generator in the repo? | **no** — it ran from a scratchpad; only the importer is committed |
| schema | `notable_majors TEXT DEFAULT '[]'` — 14 CIP families, mean **7.55 listed** |
| is it exhaustive? | **no** |
| any authoritative negative source? | **none** — no catalogue table, no CIP table, no "not offered" field |
| documented negative meaning of absence? | **none anywhere in the repository** |

**Classification: `PARTIAL_POSITIVE_ONLY`.** Proven, not assumed:

`docs/L2_ACADEMIC_CONTEXT.md` says "the programme half is essentially
complete", which is where the belief came from — but it means 98% of programmes
*carry a value*, not that the lists are catalogues. The same page then lists
family coverage: **Mathematics 6.9%, English 15.4%, Engineering 35.3%**.

The reductio is decisive. Of 349 NCAA D1 women's programmes, **321 do not list
Mathematics** — among them Michigan State, Penn State, Wisconsin, Ohio State,
Arizona State, Minnesota, Rutgers and Texas A&M. Every one of them grants
mathematics degrees. A catalogue would not say that. A list of each
institution's highest-completion families would, and does.

Separately, `docs/matchmaking-v2-spec.md` already grades `notable_majors` as
**D** — the weakest evidence grade — while `majorFit` grades a derived
non-match as `MEASURED` at `coverage: 1`.

## §D The repair contract

**Core rule: absence from partial positive evidence must not become a measured
zero.** Preserved: UNKNOWN ≠ ZERO, UNSCOREABLE ≠ BAD, NOT_APPLICABLE ≠ NON_MATCH.

| # | situation | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | no intended major | NOT_APPLICABLE | **unchanged** |
| 2 | major present in the list | value 1, MEASURED, cov 1 | **unchanged** |
| 3 | major **absent from a partial list** | **value 0, MEASURED, cov 1** | **UNSCOREABLE, new reason** |
| 4 | programme has no list | UNSCOREABLE `NO_PROGRAMME_MAJOR_EVIDENCE` | **unchanged** |
| 5 | authoritative negative evidence | — | **not implemented: no such source exists** |
| 6 | synonym match | value 1 via `majorLabelFor` | **unchanged** |
| 7 | unknown/undecided athlete major | NOT_APPLICABLE | **unchanged** |
| 8 | malformed programme evidence | UNSCOREABLE `NO_PROGRAMME_MAJOR_EVIDENCE` | **unchanged** |

Row 3 is the entire repair.

### Why UNSCOREABLE and not NOT_APPLICABLE

Both satisfy the core rule, and both produce an **identical Opportunity value** —
the layer renormalises over what scored either way. They differ only in
coverage, and therefore only in whether the coverage floor fires.

`notApplicable` means *the question does not apply to this pairing* — the
athlete stated no major. `unscoreable` means *the question applies and the
evidence is missing*. A declared major against a list that cannot settle it is
the second: the athlete did ask, and Thriv3 cannot answer.

The decisive argument is internal consistency. An **empty** list already
refuses with `NO_PROGRAMME_MAJOR_EVIDENCE`. A **short** list that omits the
major is the same epistemic situation — we cannot tell. Giving them different
states would replace one asymmetry with another. They are the same unknown and
get the same state, with different reasons so an explanation can say which.

### §F Opportunity composition — measured, not assumed

`majorFit` is **not** `required`, so `combine` renormalises rather than
refusing. `unscoreable` keeps the component in the denominator, so coverage
falls by exactly its share: `coverage_new = coverage_old − share`.

| candidate | new Opportunity coverage | cells losing rankability |
|---|---|---|
| **UNSCOREABLE** | min 0.192, median 0.455 | **19 of 558 (3.4%)** |
| NOT_APPLICABLE | min 0.547, median 1.000 | 0 |

The 19 are not an accident of the repair: every one already lacks win-rate
history, so only Playing Pathway could be assembled — 0.65 of 2.2, coverage
0.295, below the frozen `OPPORTUNITY_COVERAGE_FLOOR` of 0.35. They all
currently sit at ranks 462–714. They become LIMITED_DATA, which is a visible,
truthful state and not a low score. That is the frozen floor doing its job on a
programme whose opportunity evidence really is thin.

### §G Asymmetry

The repair gives majorFit the shape §G describes, without inventing a prior,
a floor or a probability: **a known positive match adds authority; an
unanswerable question is neutral; an authoritative negative would subtract —
and is not implemented because no such evidence exists.** A matched programme
still carries a 0.545 share at value 1, so positive major evidence keeps
meaningful authority. No weight is changed.

### §I Mutation requirement

The original defect — partial-list absence producing a measured zero — must
make a test fail.
