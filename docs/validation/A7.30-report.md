# A7.30 — independent academic recruitability validation

**Read-only. No scoring change, no boundary change, no data ingestion.** Production remains
`0ae0399`. The A7.29 shadow logic was frozen exactly and not touched.

**Independent validation WAS obtained**, with limits stated precisely below. Three separate models
in fresh contexts judged a frozen 15-card pack.

**Three headline results.** The **zero-false-positive property survives independent review** — no
card the independents called NORMAL was escalated, and there were **no over-escalations at all**.
The **falling-selectivity frontier hypothesis is confirmed**, and the true frontier is *lower* than
A7.29 suggested. And a **previously-accepted conclusion is reversed**: the shadow returns NORMAL on
a card all three independents unanimously called PRE_READ, for a reason the shadow does not model at
all.

# 1. Selection protocol and the frozen pack

Pre-registered at `fade6f5`, **before generation**. Cells defined on acceptance rate and SAT
shortfall only — quantities in the database, independent of any assessment. Best-ranked qualifying
programme per cell, ties by name, no institution reused, and **every institution used in the A7.27,
A7.28 or A7.29 packs excluded**.

One correction was made before any evaluator saw the pack and is recorded in the protocol rather
than applied quietly: the fallback rule "nearest real record" was implemented as *best-ranked within
a widened band*, which returned a −284 record for a −400 cell and cost the pack the severe-mismatch
coverage the brief requires. It now means **nearest in shortfall to the cell target**. Exclusion was
also re-keyed from programme name to **`unitid`**, because the two sports spell some institutions
differently ("MIT" against "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") and a name key let one through.

**Coverage achieved:** shortfalls −53 to −797, acceptance 4% to 90%, 9 men's and 6 women's, ability
3/6/7/9, major matching, non-matching and undeclared, net prices $6.1k–$43.8k, one international
athlete. Five cells had no record in band and fell back, each recorded.

**What is genuinely independent:**

- **Three different models**, none of which is the model that designed the boundary (Opus 5).
- **Fresh contexts.** None saw this conversation, the A7.27/A7.28/A7.29 reports, the shadow
  classifications, the boundary formula, the stepped-frontier hypothesis, or any previous rationale.
- **Identical input**, pinned by prompt digest — each was told to read one file outside the

- **Two tool calls each**, which is one read of the prompt and one hand-back. That is direct
  evidence none of them explored the repository, and it was checked rather than assumed.
- I did not control the answers. They disagree with each other on four cards and with me on several.

**What is NOT independent, stated plainly:**

- **I wrote the prompt and built the pack.** Framing is mine.
- All three are **Claude-family models** with correlated training. Model diversity is real but
  partial.
- **None is a human domain expert.** The unanimous NCAA-eligibility finding in §5 is exactly the kind
  of thing a practitioner would catch and a model might confabulate; it reads as correct to me, but
  I am not the right check on that.
- I saw the shadow labels in terminal output while generating the pack. Selection was mechanical
  under the pre-registered rule, so this could not steer which cards were chosen — but it is
  disclosed rather than omitted.

**Verdict: this is genuine independent evaluation of the judgement, and it is not a substitute for
human expert review.** It clears the A7.29 blocker on operator visibility. It does not clear
athlete-facing visibility.

# 5. False positives — **zero, independently confirmed**

**No card the independents called NORMAL was escalated by the shadow, and the shadow never assigned
a level above the independent majority on any card.** This is the property that has mattered most
since A7.28, and it is the one that survives contact with judges who did not design it.

**Six of seven are the A7.29 defect, measured larger.** Internal review found 5 under-escalations in

## The seventh is a reversal, and the reason is not in the model at all

**Old Dominion: shortfall −797, acceptance 90%, athlete GPA 1.0 / SAT 400. The shadow returns
NORMAL. All three independents returned PRE_READ, unanimously, and all three gave the same reason:
a 1.0 GPA falls below the NCAA Division I initial-eligibility core-GPA floor, which is a national
rule that no admissions office and no coach can waive.**

The shadow's high-acceptance clearance — the rule that has produced its zero-false-positive record —
is correct about *institutional admission* and blind to *athletic eligibility*. At a 90%-acceptance
institution the admissions office is not the obstacle; the NCAA is.

**This reverses my own A7.29 verdict.** On the analogous control there I answered NORMAL and wrote
that the eligibility question "is a different system, it is not on this card, and it should not be
smuggled in as an institutional-admissions concern." Three independent evaluators disagreed
unanimously, and on the question actually being asked — *what action should be taken before treating
this as a straightforward recruitment option* — they are right. An athlete who cannot be eligible is
not a straightforward option, whatever the admissions office would say.

**Consequence for the product, and it is the most important line in this report: the absence of a
flag must never be presented as academic clearance.** The shadow does not model NCAA initial
eligibility, and a silent NORMAL on a 1.0 GPA is exactly how that gap would mislead an operator.

# 7. Is VERIFY_EARLY a genuine middle action?

**Yes, but it is used more sparingly than internal review suggested.** Independents assigned it 8
times in 45 individual judgements (18%) and it won the majority on only 2 of 14 cards. All three
models articulated the same distinction unprompted, in their own words:

> *"VERIFY_EARLY means the athlete is plausibly admissible through the coach's normal channels and
> the gap is something an early conversation can settle… PRE_READ means the answer from admissions
> could plausibly be no, so the family should not build a plan around the programme until that
> answer is in hand."* — Sonnet 5

> *"the difference is whether the school can carry weight in the family's list while the answer is
> still outstanding."* — Fable 5.1

Three models that had never seen the A7.29 definitions converged on the same operational
distinction. **The two levels are real.** But the middle band is narrower than the shadow assumes:
independents used VERIFY only at 29% and 40% acceptance.

# 8. The falling-selectivity frontier — **confirmed, and lower than hypothesised**

The shadow requires a shortfall of ≥350 to reach PRE_READ at *any* acceptance rate below 50%. The
independent majority:

| acceptance | PRE_READ at shortfalls | VERIFY at | NORMAL at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4–10%** | 283, 284, 285, 434 | — | — |
| **10–20%** | 270, 299, 408 | — | — |
| **20–30%** | 315, 460 | 296 | — |
| **30–45%** | 407 | 319 | — |
| **>50%** | 797 *(eligibility, not admissions)* | — | +300 |

**Monotone, and the frontier sits well below −350 everywhere under 30% acceptance.** At single-digit
acceptance the independents escalated at shortfalls as small as 283; at 20–30% the frontier lies
between 296 and 315; at 30–45% between 319 and 407.

**A7.29's hypothesis was directionally right and quantitatively conservative.** Its own frontier read
≈250 at 5–10% and 316–417 at 20–30%; independent judgement puts the 20–30% frontier at ~300, not
~400.

**The boundary was NOT modified**, as instructed. This is evidence about the shape, recorded for the
next phase.

# 9. The Harvard split — a vocabulary problem, not an evidence problem

U-15 is the only card without a majority, and it divided three ways: NORMAL / VERIFY / PRE_READ. A
4.0 GPA and 1500 SAT, 53 points below Harvard's enrolled average, at 4% acceptance.

The three rationales show the disagreement is **not about the athlete**:

- *Fable*: no academic obstacle exists → NORMAL.
- *Opus*: the Ivy pre-read is a standard early step → VERIFY.
- *Sonnet*: the formal pre-read is a procedural requirement for every recruit → PRE_READ.

All three agree the athlete is academically fine. They disagree about whether a **procedural step
that happens to every recruit regardless of strength** should be reported as a concern. **The
current vocabulary cannot express "no concern, but this institution type requires a formal step
anyway"**, and the split is the vocabulary failing rather than the evidence being thin. Worth
resolving before the flag is shown to anyone.

# 10. GPA — all three used it, and one of them explained why that matters

Every evaluator reported weighing GPA alongside SAT rather than behind it, and it was **decisive on
Old Dominion for all three**. Two also used it on UCF, to *hold the level down* rather than raise it
— a 2.8 GPA clearing the NCAA sliding scale was the reason UCF stayed at VERIFY.

One observation is worth quoting because it connects two threads:

> *"Every card listed test policy as NOT ON FILE, so I could not assume the SAT would even be read;
> GPA is the one academic signal certain to be evaluated."* — Fable 5.1

That is a sharper argument for collecting `test_policy` than anything in A7.29, and it also
undermines the current design from the other side: **the shadow scores only the SAT, and the one
signal that is always read is the one it ignores.** GPA remains unmodelled and unscored in this
phase, as instructed, but "context only" is now a weaker position than it was.

The programme-side gap is unchanged and still binding: there is **no programme-side GPA evidence
anywhere** for any of 2,406 programmes.

# 11. What the independents used that the shadow has no model of

Unprompted, all three reasoned about **institution-type admissions mechanics** the shadow does not
represent: the Ivy Academic Index, Patriot League banding, NESCAC slot rationing, service-academy
congressional nomination, and Berea's tuition-free mission-based admissions. Two independently
identified USMMA's nomination requirement as making its 34% acceptance rate misleading, and two
identified Berea's 19% as not being conventional selectivity.

**This is a richer signal than shortfall × acceptance, and it is the most promising direction the
phase surfaced.** It is also exactly what `programme_academic_guidance` and `observed_supported_band`
were designed to hold (A7.28 §12).

# 13. Feature gate — **GATE 2**

**Operator-visible intelligence, no ranking influence.**

Justified because: the zero-false-positive property held under independent review; the two levels are
independently confirmed as distinct actions; NORMAL/CONCERN separated on 13 of 14; and the one
failure is a **false negative**, which leaves the operator no worse off than today, where they are
never warned at all.

**GATE 2 is granted with two conditions, both from this phase:**

1. **The absence of a flag must not be presented as academic clearance.** Required wording near any
   surface: *"Thriv3 checks institutional admissions evidence only. It does not check NCAA or NAIA
   initial eligibility."*
2. **The Harvard-type procedural case must be resolvable**, or a fourth state added, before the flag
   is shown anywhere a procedural pre-read would be mistaken for a concern.

**GATE 3 is not approached.** Ranking influence needs separate evidence, and the boundary now has a
confirmed shape defect and a confirmed hole.

# 15. Smallest next step

**Re-shape the boundary, in one preregistered phase, against the independent evidence already in
hand** — not against new packs. Specifically:

1. Fit the **stepped acceptance bands** proposed in A7.29 §9 to the 14 independent majorities, which
   is the first evidence not generated by the boundary's own author.
2. Add an **NCAA/NAIA initial-eligibility check as a separate signal**, not as part of the
   admissions shortfall. It is a different mechanism with different data (athlete GPA against a
   published sliding scale) and it must not be folded into the acceptance-rate rule that correctly
   clears open-admission institutions.
3. Resolve the **procedural-pre-read case**, either by a fourth state or by scoping the vocabulary
   to academic *concern* only.

**`test_policy` ingestion remains a separate data task** and was not started, as instructed.

# 16. Unresolved operator decisions

1. **Is a human expert review required before GATE 2 ships?** Three models are not a practitioner.
   The NCAA-eligibility finding in particular deserves one.
2. **Fourth state, or scope the vocabulary?** The Harvard split needs a product answer.
3. **Does Thriv3 want to model NCAA/NAIA initial eligibility at all?** It is a real gate, the data

   viability.
4. **Where does the operator-facing flag live**, and does a flagged programme still receive outreach.
5. **Should GPA now be modelled?** The "one signal certain to be read" argument is new and strong.
6. **Recruited-athlete latitude** — still unknown, still the load-bearing gap.
