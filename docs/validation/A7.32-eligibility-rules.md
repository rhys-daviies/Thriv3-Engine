# A7.32 — initial academic eligibility: sourced rule register

**Research and specification only. No rule implemented, no code written, no scoring changed.**

**Verification date for every entry: 2026-09-26.** Every rule carries its source. Following
`shared/eligibility.js`: *"A rule with no source is not a rule."*

**Scope: INITIAL academic eligibility** — what an entering student-athlete must satisfy to be
eligible to compete. **Not** institutional admission, **not** continuing/progress-toward-degree,
**not** transfer, **not** amateurism, **not** financial aid.

## 1. NCAA Division I — VERIFIED

| field | value |
|---|---|
| **core courses** | **16**, distributed: English/native language 4 · Math (Algebra I or higher) 3 · Science 2 · additional English/Math/Science 1 · Social Science 2 · additional core units (may include world language, comparative religion, philosophy) 4 |
| **core-course GPA** | **minimum 2.300** |
| **standardized test** | **NOT REQUIRED** |
| **timing rule (10/7)** | *"Ten of your core courses (7 in English, math, or science) must be completed before start of the seventh semester (senior year)."* |
| **graduation** | *"Submit Final Transcript and Proof of Graduation."* Students who graduate on time *"may be able to use one additional NCAA-approved core course within one year after high school graduation."* |
| **certifying body** | NCAA Eligibility Center |

**Test-score removal — exact provenance.** NCAA Division I Proposal **2022-34**, *"Academic
Eligibility — Freshman Academic Requirements — Elimination of Test-Score Requirements"*, status
**Adopted Final**, effective **1 August 2023** for student-athletes initially enrolling full-time on
or after that date. Amended **Bylaw 14.3.1.1**; the qualifier standard is now only *"a minimum
cumulative grade-point average as specified in Bylaw 14.3.1.1.3 of 2.300"* in core courses. The
former test-score subsection was removed entirely.

> **This is the rule A7.31 warned must not be taken from memory.** The historic sliding scale
> (core GPA traded against SAT/ACT) **no longer exists** for Division I.

**CAVEAT — practice / competition / aid distinction.** Division I historically distinguished
*qualifier*, *academic redshirt* and *nonqualifier*, each permitting different combinations of
practice, athletics aid and competition. **The ncaa.org requirements page does not state these
categories, and the fact-sheet PDF could not be retrieved.** Whether an academic-redshirt category
still exists post-2022-34, and at what threshold, is **NOT ESTABLISHED** and must be verified before
any implementation.

**Sources:** [NCAA DI initial eligibility requirements](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/initial-eligibility-requirements/division-i/) · [NCAA Legislative Services Database, Proposal 2022-34](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=106367)

## 3. NCAA Division III — VERIFIED, and it is a different kind of answer

> *"Division III schools set their own academic standards on campus, but the Eligibility Center
> certifies the athletics eligibility of Division III international student-athletes."*

| field | value |
|---|---|
| **NCAA-wide academic threshold** | **NONE. There is no national Division III initial academic standard.** |
| **who sets the standard** | the institution |
| **international students** | **certified by the NCAA Eligibility Center** — *"including first-year enrollees and transfers"* |

**This is the single most important structural finding of the phase.** For a domestic Division III
athlete the academic question is **entirely dimension A (institutional admissions)** and dimension B
does not exist. For an **international** Division III athlete **both dimensions exist at once** —
which is precisely Thriv3's New Zealand population.

**Source:** [NCAA initial eligibility requirements](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/initial-eligibility-requirements/)

## 5. New Zealand — PARTIAL

The NCAA publishes a **country-specific** requirement page for New Zealand. Retrieved via the NCAA
search index; the PDF itself returned connection errors on every attempt and the ncaa.org New
Zealand page now 302-redirects to the homepage.

**What the index reports, marked as PARTIAL and requiring confirmation before use:**

- **NCEA Level 2** is an acceptable form of **proof of high school graduation**, as a certificate or
  as the **New Zealand Record of Achievement** showing Level 2 achieved under the *Qualification and
  Achievement Summary* section.
- **NCEA Level 3** is also acceptable, in certificate form or as the Record of Achievement.
- Translations are not an issue since documents are already in English.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** whether **University Entrance** is required or mentioned; the treatment of
**Cambridge International / CIE**; the treatment of the **International Baccalaureate**; and how
NCEA achievement standards map to NCAA core courses.

> **No conversion table was built and none should be.** The Eligibility Center performs the
> certification. Thriv3 replicating a conversion would be manufacturing a number that the only
> authority to issue it has not issued.

**Sources:** [NCAA New Zealand country requirements (PDF, index entry)](http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/International_Information/Guide/Countries/NewZealand.pdf) · [NCAA Guide to International Academic Standards](http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/International_Information/International_Guide.pdf)

## 6. NAIA — PARTIAL

The bylaw page `naia.org/legislative/handbook/article-5-section-c` returned **HTTP 404** on direct
fetch. What follows is consistent across NAIA-controlled sources (the naia.org search index and
`interpretations.naia.org`) and is recorded as **PARTIAL**.

**Standing rule — Article V, Section C, Item 2.** An entering freshman must meet **two of three**:

1. overall high-school GPA ≥ **2.000** on a 4.000 scale
2. finish in the **top half** of the graduating class
3. **ACT ≥ 18** or **SAT ≥ 970** (Critical Reading + Math only; Writing excluded; *"Super Scores"
   are not accepted*; test taken before the term in which the student wishes to compete)

**Additional standalone pathway.** From **1 May 2022** (entering freshmen from **fall 2022**), a
student may satisfy initial eligibility by the **elevated GPA alone: 2.300 on a 4.000 scale**.

Also recorded: an entering freshman must be *"a graduate of an accredited high school or be accepted
as a regular student in good standing as defined by the enrolling institution."* **The NAIA has no
core-course requirement** — the GPA is the overall high-school GPA, not a core-course GPA. That is a
structural difference from the NCAA and matters for Thriv3's inputs.

**Certifying body:** the NAIA Eligibility Center (**PlayNAIA**).

> **Two independent readings encountered during this research conflicted** — one NAIA source phrased
> the alternative as "1 of 3" and another as "2 of 3". The reconciliation above (2-of-3 standing
> rule, plus a 2.300 standalone pathway added in 2022) is consistent with both but **was not read
> off the bylaw text** and must be confirmed.

**Sources:** [NAIA Bylaws Article V, Section C](https://www.naia.org/legislative/handbook/article-5-section-c) (404 on fetch) · [NAIA interpretations — Basics of Freshman Eligibility](https://interpretations.naia.org/basics-of-freshman-eligibility/) (cites Article V, Section C, Item 2 and the 2025-26 Official Handbook, p. 70)

## 8. USCAA — UNVERIFIED / INSUFFICIENT PRIMARY EVIDENCE

**A naming hazard was found and must be recorded.** The domain **`uscaa.org` is the United States
CORPORATE Athletics Association** — a corporate track-and-field body with no connection to college
sport. A domain-restricted search returned its rules about employee payroll status and alumni
scoring. **Encoding those as collegiate eligibility would have been a serious error**, and it is
exactly the failure mode a "no rule without a source" discipline exists to catch.

The collegiate body is the **United States Collegiate Athletic Association** at **`theuscaa.com`**.
Its eligibility page returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden**.

Search-index material suggested a high-school diploma or GED plus full-time enrolment (12 credit
hours), with GPA standards attaching **after** 24 credit hours — i.e. mostly **continuing**
eligibility. **Not recorded as a rule.**

**Source attempted:** [USCAA eligibility rules](https://theuscaa.com/Information/Eligibility_Rules)
