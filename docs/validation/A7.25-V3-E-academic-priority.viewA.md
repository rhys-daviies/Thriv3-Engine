# A7.25 BLIND LIST REVIEW — A7.25-V3-E-academic-priority

**This is a blind review.** Below are THREE ordered outreach lists for the same athlete,
drawn from the same universe of programmes on the same day. Nothing on this page tells you
where any list came from, which one anybody currently uses, or what distinguishes them.
The letters A, B and C carry no meaning and are not in any order.

You are judging **the programme recommendations**, not a method. For each list, say which
schools belong in the first wave of outreach for this athlete, which do not, and which
trade-offs the list gets wrong. There is no score to give and no ranking of the lists to
produce — if two lists are equally good, or equally bad, say so.

The programmes are described once, in the appendix at the end, under a fixed code. The codes
are in an arbitrary order and mean nothing.

### What this athlete asked for

- Competing at the highest realistic college level: **3 of 5**
- A clearer path to playing time: **3 of 5**
- A college with a strong academic profile: **5 of 5**
- Intended major: **Engineering**

Scale: **1** = matters materially less to this athlete than it would by default · **3** =
**neutral / default importance** · **5** = matters substantially more than default. A low
number is NOT a request for the opposite: an athlete who rates academic strength 1 has not
asked for a weak university.

## What to weigh

Judge each list the way a recruitment adviser would, on all of:

- the standard of football the athlete can realistically play at;
- whether a coach at that programme would plausibly recruit them;
- whether there is a genuine pathway to playing;
- whether the family can afford it;
- academic strength and, where the athlete has declared one, the intended major;
- anything else in the facts that bears on fit.

A list can be wrong by being too timid about what the athlete asked for, and it can be wrong
by chasing it past the point of good advice. Both are real answers.
