## 2. The exact current rule — Article V, Section C, Item 2

**Item 1 (p. 70).** An entering freshman must be a graduate of an accredited high school, *or* be
accepted as a regular student in good standing as defined by the enrolling institution.

**Item 2 (p. 70).** Two alternative pathways:

- **Pathway A — standalone.** An overall high-school GPA of **2.300** or higher on a 4.000 scale
  makes an entering freshman eligible immediately. No test, no class rank.
- **Pathway B — two of the three** (pp. 71–72):

| | criterion | threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **2a** | standardized test | **ACT ≥ 18** or **SAT ≥ 970**, Evidence-Based Reading and Writing + Math, for tests from **1 May 2019** |
| **2b** | overall high-school GPA | **≥ 2.000** on a 4.000 scale |
| **2c** | class rank | **upper half** of the graduating class, on the final transcript after graduation |

**There is no core-course requirement.** The GPA is the *overall* high-school GPA. This is a
structural difference from the NCAA, not a threshold difference.

**Also recorded, not encoded:** Item 2b's early-determination exception (6 semesters at 3.000 + test;
7 semesters at 2.500 + test); the 2c exception for nine institutional credit hours at "C" or better;
GED/HiSet and home-school notes.

## 3. The A7.32 conflict, resolved — and it was never a conflict in NAIA material

A7.32 recorded that "two independent readings conflicted", one saying **1 of 3** and one **2 of 3**,
and left the rule PARTIAL for that reason. Reading the sources literally:

| source | what it literally says |
|---|---|
| **Bylaw, Item 2** | *"…can become eligible by meeting two of the three entry-level requirements below."* |
| **interpretations.naia.org**, raw HTML | *"…or meet the 'traditional' 2/3 options described below"* |
| **International guide** | *"…a 2.3+ GPA OR meet two of the following three criteria"* |

**All three agree: two of three.** Both of the conflicting readings were produced by *summarisers
standing between the phase and the text* — including, in this phase, a fresh page-summary that again
rendered it as "1-of-3" while the page underneath said 2/3. The discipline that a rule must come from
primary text is the only reason this did not ship twice.

The two pathways **coexist** rather than compete: 2.300 alone, **or** two of the three. The "2.300
pathway" is not an amendment that replaced the traditional test; it sits beside it in the same item.

### Two places A7.32's PARTIAL record was wrong

1. **SAT sections.** A7.32 recorded *"Critical Reading and Math only; Writing excluded"* — the
   pre-2016 section names, taken from the interpretations page, which still carries them. **The
   bylaw says Evidence-Based Reading and Writing, and Math**, for tests from May 2019. The bylaw
   governs; the corrected wording is encoded and a test asserts the old wording is absent.
2. **Effective date.** A7.32 dated the 2.300 pathway to 1 May 2022. The operative text states no
   commencement date for Item 2, and the handbook's own history line for this bylaw ends at 2021.
   **No commencement date is encoded**, and `effectiveFrom: null` is asserted as deliberate.

## 4. Standardized-test status — live, unlike the NCAA

The NCAA removed test scores from initial eligibility entirely (Proposal 2022-34, effective
2023-08-01). **The NAIA did not.** A test remains one of the three alternatives, and can be decisive.

But no athlete is *obliged* to sit one: 2.300 alone, or GPA + class rank, both suffice. A single
boolean cannot express that, so the record carries two fields — `standardizedTestRequired: false`
and `standardizedTestRole: 'ONE OF THE THREE ALTERNATIVES…'`.

**The test criterion is not a score, it is a score with provenance:** a single sitting, on a national,
international or official state testing date, no super-scoring, taken before the term of first
participation.

## 5. Domestic-athlete process

The Section C preamble is explicit: any first-time NAIA participant **must receive an eligible
determination from the NAIA Eligibility Center before competing** against outside competition. The
institution then verifies graduation and all other requirements. Certification is not the school's
and is certainly not Thriv3's.

## 6. International-athlete process — the part that matters most to Thriv3

**NOTE 3, p. 73.** An incoming freshman who graduated outside the US or its territories meets **the
same criteria** (Items 1 and 2). High-school graduation and cumulative GPA *"shall be determined
based on current published NAIA initial-eligibility academic guidelines for international students."*

From the Eligibility Center's international guide:

- Registration at **PlayNAIA.org**, plus a purchased **InCred** evaluation of international records.
- **The NAIA converts grades to a U.S. 4.000 scale itself.** The required number is one the NAIA
  produces. Nothing Thriv3 holds can stand in for it.
- Test scores must be sent directly to the Eligibility Center; international testing dates apply.
- Class rank needs a letter from a secondary-school official on letterhead.
- **Home-schooled, "Private User" (England and British-patterned systems), distance-education and
  international GED students may not use class rank at all** and must meet the test *and* GPA
  criteria.

> **No country conversion is encoded, and no country is named anywhere in the rule record** — a test
> asserts that, checking for "New Zealand", "NCEA", "A-Level" and others. Which countries the
> "Private User" category covers is **NOT ESTABLISHED** and must not be inferred from a country name.
> The country-by-country guidelines are a separate publication that was not retrieved; that gap is
> recorded as a caveat on the rule.

## 9. Thriv3 input-gap analysis — the rule is known, the evidence is not

| the bylaw needs | Thriv3 holds | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **final** overall HS GPA on a 4.000 scale, from the final transcript **after graduation** | `players.gpa` — a self-reported number typed into a 0–4 box, commonly years before that transcript exists; no verification, no weighted/unweighted rule, no multi-school or prep-school handling | **same name, different quantity** |
| a score at a **single approved sitting**, no super-score, before the term | `sat_score`, `act_score` — bare integers, no sitting, no date. The product also converts ACT→SAT **for admissions**, which the NAIA does not do | **provenance absent** |
| **class rank** in the upper half | **no field exists anywhere in the schema** | **absent entirely** |
| proof of graduation | nothing | absent |
| NAIA Eligibility Center determination | nothing | absent |

**Class rank having no field is decisive**: at most one of the three could ever be attempted, so
**Pathway B is unreachable by construction, not by choice**. And Pathway A needs a verified final
transcript GPA that Thriv3 does not hold.

Per §6 of the brief, no GPA or test field was turned into an eligibility input. The assessor still
takes **no GPA, SAT or ACT parameter at all** — the guarantee is structural, and a test passes those
values in directly to prove they are inert.

**This is the only behavioural change, and it is the sanctioned one.** The two states say different
things: `RULE_UNVERIFIED` means *we do not know the rule*; `NOT_ESTABLISHED` means *we know it and
lack the athlete's evidence*. After A7.35 the first is false and the second is true. **No state
asserts eligibility, and `VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE` still does not exist.** The result contract is unchanged:
`NOT_APPLICABLE`, `NOT_ESTABLISHED`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `NO_ISSUE_IDENTIFIED`, `RULE_UNVERIFIED`.

**One latent defect was fixed in passing.** The `NOT_ESTABLISHED` reason was a single sentence shared
by all associations, ending *"A transcript GPA is not a core-course GPA, and standardized test scores
are not part of this standard."* **Both halves are false of the NAIA** — it has no core courses, and
tests *are* part of its standard. The reason now comes from the rule record, so an explanation can
never outlive the association it was written for. A test asserts the NAIA reason contains neither
phrase and the NCAA reasons still contain theirs.
