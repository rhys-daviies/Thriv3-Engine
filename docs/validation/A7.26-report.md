## 4. Academic scoring dependency graph

```
academic_rating ─▶ academicPercentileScale ─▶ academicPercentile ─┐
                                                                  ├─▶ academicStrengthFit ─┐
academic_strength_priority ─▶ priorityStrength ───────────────────┘                        │
                           └─▶ ambitionMultiplier ─▶ component weight ─────────────────────┤

gpa, sat_score, act_score, academic_minimum ─▶ ✗ nothing
sat_avg, admit_rate                         ─▶ ✗ nothing
```

**`academicStrengthFit = (1 − strength(p)) + strength(p) · percentile`.** At priority 1 the strength
term is 0, so the component is a **flat 1.000 for every programme** and reorders nothing. That fact
is the instrument this phase uses throughout: comparing priority 1 with priority 5 isolates the
entire contribution of institutional academic strength.

The layer's own docstring already names the gap: *"It reads ONE input. Not GPA, not SAT, not ACT,
not admit rate, not net price, not the intended major … three of them are not modelled at all yet."*

## 7. Major-ordering audit — `majorFit` is never overridden from inside Opportunity

Every pair where a non-matching programme ranks ≥10 places above a matching one, in the top 100,
decomposed exactly:

```
ΔP = M_n·(0.5ΔR + 0.2ΔF + 0.3ΔO) + base_m·(M_n − M_m)        M = gR · gF · levelFactor
```

(identity verified exact to 1e-9 on every row.)

| | V3-E | W3-E |
|---|---|---|
| qualifying pairs | 489 | 374 |
| **A — justified override** | **0** | **0** |
| **B — questionable override** | **0** | **0** |
| **C — stronger non-academic evidence** | **388 (79.3%)** | **345 (92.2%)** |
| D — insufficient evidence | 101 | 29 |
| dominant cause | **level anchor 52%** · R 21% · F 17% | **level anchor 48%** · R 34% · F 17% |

**A and B are zero for a structural reason, not by accident: the Opportunity term is negative in
863 of 863 pairs** (max −0.040). A non-matching programme can never win on Opportunity — median O is
0.772 for matching against 0.356 for non-matching, which is `majorFit` 1.20 doing exactly what A7.23
designed. Every override comes from the layers that are supposed to hold ordering authority.

**This corrects a claim I made in the A7.25 blind review** — *"every list ranks colleges that do not
teach Engineering above colleges that do, on the strength of a rating."* Measured: no override is
driven by rating. Matching programmes are also the academically **stronger** group here (median
percentile 0.697 vs 0.489), so the major and academic quality are aligned, not in tension.

Two things follow. The preference **is** elevating expensive academic programmes — 8 to 24 places —

## 10. `playingPathway` confound — confirmed, with the mechanism

Over all twelve profiles' top 100 (n = 1200):

| pathway band | n | median strength | squad at position | eligible to remain | departing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ≥ 0.80 | 130 | **45.7** | 7 | **1** | 3 |
| 0.60–0.80 | 496 | 56.8 | 8 | 2 | 3 |
| 0.40–0.60 | 504 | **70.4** | 10 | 5 | 3 |
| < 0.40 | 70 | 64.2 | 12 | 7 | 3 |

```
corr(pathway, programme strength)          = −0.349
corr(pathway, players eligible to remain)  = −0.696      ← the mechanism
corr(pathway, squad size at the position)  = −0.477
corr(pathway, players departing)           = +0.058      ← NOT the mechanism
```

**High pathway means a thin squad with few returners — not a lot of departures.** Departures are
flat at a median of 3 across every band. A weak programme carrying 7 at the position with 1 staying
scores above 0.80; a strong programme carrying 12 with 7 staying scores below 0.40. Programme
strength differs by **25 points** between the top and middle bands.

Confirmed and **recorded, not repaired**, as instructed. Added to the open-issues register below.

## 12. Where it belongs, reasoned from semantics

**What does the signal actually tell us?** *Given this athlete's record and this institution's
admissions evidence, would this pursuit be able to proceed at all?* That is not a preference, not a
cost, and not a statement about programme quality.

| candidate | verdict |
|---|---|
| **Eligibility** | **No.** Eligibility is a hard binary that removes programmes. Admission is probabilistic, and a recruited athlete genuinely does get latitude we cannot size. Deleting a programme on an inference repeats the BAD-4 "major as eligibility" error the A7.21 controls were built to reject. |
| **Recruitability** | **Yes — this is the semantic home.** Recruitability already answers "would this coach pursue this athlete, and would it go anywhere". At an academically selective institution the **academic pre-read is a formal step in the recruiting process**: a coach does not spend a slot on an athlete who cannot clear admissions. It sits exactly parallel to `athleticPlausibility` (can they play at this level) and `positionalSupport` (is there a place for them). |
| **Financial** | **No.** Cost and admission are different questions and A7.25 specifically warned against collapsing them. |
| **Opportunity** | **No, twice over.** Opportunity describes what the athlete wants and would get; admission is not a want. And A7.20 established Opportunity has no ordering authority (≤0.30 of base), so the signal would be inert there. |
| **Pursuit gate** | Mechanically available, and the `tailGate` shape already exists — but it would double-count if Recruitability carries the signal. Keep as the extreme-case instrument only. |

**Caution against the obvious implementation.** Recruitability is `R = A × (φ + (1−φ)·support)` with
`support = 0.75·positional + 0.25·market`. Adding admissions as a third support term changes
Candidate G's semantics, which A7.18 validated and A7.19 re-validated on both sports. If the signal
goes into Recruitability it should be reasoned about as a **separate factor**, not folded into
`support` — but that is a design question for a later phase, not a conclusion of this one.

## 13. Rank, flag, or gate? — **flag always, gate only the extreme tail, do not rank**

**Do not rank.** Three reasons, all measured here:

1. **Coverage forbids it.** 54.7% of men's programmes carry both quantities and **23.2% carry
   neither**. A signal that ranked on this would systematically sort the measured against the
   unmeasured — the exact failure A7.16–A7.18 spent two phases fixing for `positionalSupport`.
2. **The exposure is concentrated.** Zero for four of six men's profiles; the whole effect lives on
   one athlete type. Spreading an uncalibrated adjustment across every list to fix 12 placements is
   the wrong ratio.
3. **We cannot calibrate the magnitude.** How much latitude a recruited athlete gets is precisely
   what we do not know, and A7.25's lesson is that amplifying a signal we have not validated makes
   recommendations worse.

**Flag — yes, wherever the evidence is MEASURED.** "This institution's average SAT is about 400
points above yours and it admits 6% of applicants" is true, useful, actionable, and costs **nothing**
in ordering authority. It is available today for 74–84 of the top 100 on most profiles.

**Gate — only the extreme tail, and only on MEASURED evidence.** The `tailGate(v, {floor, threshold})`
shape is already in production and understood. A floor well above zero would attenuate the handful of
genuinely implausible placements without excluding anything. Where evidence is PARTIAL or
UNSCOREABLE the factor must be exactly 1 — never a penalty for missing data, which is the
`positionalSupport` lesson restated.

## 14. Smallest next experiment

**Do not design the signal yet. Establish what latitude is real.** Two read-only steps, in order:

1. **Widen the athlete set before widening the architecture.** The exposure sweep above is
   arithmetic on six lists. Run the twelve profiles with athlete SAT/GPA varied across the realistic
   intake range — this costs nothing extra, since the list does not depend on them — and report the
   exposure distribution over athlete academic profiles rather than over fixtures. **This is one
   harness run and it is already possible today.**
2. **Then one blind review, of the exposed set only.** Take the V3-E / W3-E programmes carrying a
   ≥200-point measured shortfall and put them in front of recruitment judgement with the admissions
   facts visible, asking whether each belongs. A7.25's lesson is that four phases assumed a defect
   without asking; this asks before designing. A single pack, not a tournament.

**Do not preregister a scoring formula for admissions viability until step 2 returns.**

## 15. Unresolved operator decisions

1. **How much admissions latitude does a recruited athlete actually get?** This is the load-bearing
   unknown and it is a product/domain judgement, not a measurement we hold. Until it is decided,
   neither a gate threshold nor a flag wording can be specified. It is a different number for a
   D3 NESCAC pre-read than for an NAIA programme, and we model neither.
2. **Should the product show a programme the athlete probably cannot enter?** There is a defensible
   answer either way — a reach list is a real recruiting instrument — but it must be a decision,
   because today it happens silently.
3. **Re-pin the V1 matching snapshot?** The women's pool drift is legitimate and additive. Belongs
   with `fix/institution-identity-integrity`, not here.
4. **The two Phase 3D follow-ups**, carried unresolved: Emmanuel (MA) and New England College men's
   CCC against women's GNAC; and the women's Little East / GNAC conference completeness backfill
   (peer members carry NULL).
5. **Is `majorFit` 1.20 doing too much or exactly enough?** This phase shows it is never overridden
   from inside Opportunity, which is the anti-filter property holding — but it also means the major
   is effectively decisive within the layer. That was the A7.23 design intent; it is worth confirming
   it is still the intent now that it is measured.
