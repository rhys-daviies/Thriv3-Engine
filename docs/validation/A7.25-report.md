# A7.25 — blind recruitment validation of preference authority

**Nothing was implemented. No scoring file changed.** Production remains `0ae0399` (Candidate G +
`majorFit` 1.20). P4 stays withdrawn.

**Headline: the assumption A7.20–A7.24 was built on is not supported.** Judged blind on the
recommendations themselves, production was the preferred list for BOTH athletes, and both stronger
variants were worse. Raising playing or academic authority did not buy better recruitment advice —
it bought worse programmes at higher prices.

---

## 1. The blinded variants

Identical production pipeline in all six lists — same layers, weights, gates, calibration,
Candidate G, `majorFit` 1.20. One term differs, applied to Pursuit after the gates, before the
sort:

```
P' = P × (1 − τ · (1 − v))          v = the focal component value, on [0,1]
```

Non-inverting: the factor never exceeds 1, so a weak signal is never rewarded.

| variant | τ | V3-C focal | V3-E focal |
|---|---|---|---|
| control | — | — | — |
| moderate | 0.40 | `playingPathway` | `academicStrengthFit` |
| strong | 0.70 | `playingPathway` | `academicStrengthFit` |

Applied only to the athlete's own stated-5 preference. Because both athletes state that preference
at 5, **this phase does not settle the τ(3) disagreement** between A7.21 and A7.24.

## 2. Proof the evaluator was blind

| commit | what |
|---|---|
| `6c649dd` | design frozen — variants, τ, focal signals, selection rules, before any output existed |
| `87641df` | packs generated; mapping written **only** to the sealed files |
| `b54324b` | **both reviews committed**, `sealedOpened: false` |
| `28ca0a1` | **pairwise judgements committed**, `sealedOpened: false` |
| — | sealed files opened only after `28ca0a1` |

The mapping is derived from `sha256([packId, 'variant-order', variantId])`, never printed. Blind
codes come from a separate hash and track no ranking. The pack object handed to the renderer
carries no variant id, no τ and no model state, which the generator asserts structurally.

**The honest limit, restated from the design.** The generator and the evaluator are the same agent.
This is not the blindness of an independent human reviewer. What the commit chain proves is that no
judgement was written with the mapping visible. It cannot prove that a visibly preference-heavy list
was not *inferred* to be a non-control — and §11 records a case where exactly that inference went
wrong, which is itself evidence the reviews were written from content rather than from a guess.

## 6. The Financial trade-off — the answer the evidence gives

> *For an academically focused athlete, when should academic quality justify ranking a financially
> harder programme above a cheaper alternative?*

**When the athlete would plausibly be ADMITTED and the programme offers the DECLARED MAJOR. Both
conditions, not either.**

| case | net price vs $13,000 ceiling | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Calvin** — rating 7.3, SAT 1233, 71% admit, Engineering | +$9,992 | **worth it** |
| **Carleton** — rating 9.4, 20% admit, **no Engineering** | +$12,407 | **not worth it** |
| **Bowdoin** — rating 9.6, 7% admit, **no Engineering** | +$1,398 | **not worth it** |

Carleton is rated higher than Calvin and is the worse recommendation. Bowdoin is nearly the same
price as the cheap options and is still wrong. **The price is not what decides it.**

**And the measured consequence of raising academic authority.** Across the top 100, τ 0 → 0.70:

| | control | moderate | strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| median academic rating | 4.7 | 6.4 | **7.3** |
| median net price | $19,058 | $20,653 | **$23,550** |
| over the $13,000 ceiling | 83 | 93 | **97 of 100** |
| median Financial | 0.573 | 0.524 | **0.473** |
| median programme strength | 50.7 | 49.9 | 48.3 |
| >8 points below level | 11 | 26 | **40** |

The 52 programmes the strong variant brings in have median academic rating **7.75** and median net
price **$25,467**; the 52 it removes have rating **3.40** and net price **$15,434**. The direction
is exactly right and the cost is exactly the affordable half of the list. This is A7.24's
`corr(academic rating, F) = −0.270` expressed as a ranking.

## 7. The playing-pathway trade-off

Across the top 100, τ 0 → 0.70:

| | control | moderate | strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| median `playingPathway` | 0.567 | 0.710 | **0.752** |
| median programme strength (athlete: 62.1) | 56.5 | 54.7 | **52.3** |
| >8 points below level | 31 | 47 | **56 of 100** |
| median Recruitability | 0.763 | 0.752 | **0.716** |
| median Financial | 0.878 | 0.857 | **0.767** |
| over the $18,000 ceiling | 54 | 54 | 60 |

The 48 entrants have median pathway **0.812** against the exits' **0.512** — the term does exactly
what it claims — and median strength **46.2** against **55.9**, Financial **0.674** against
**0.941**, Recruitability **0.676** against **0.765**.

**`playingPathway` is confounded with programme weakness.** A nearly empty forward room at a losing
D3 scores maximally: Beloit has six forwards, five departing and none eligible to remain, which is
why τ = 0.70 lifts it to #7 at strength 34.0. **Buying playing authority through this signal buys
level, recruitability and affordability damage simultaneously, and that is a property of the
evidence, not of the multiplier.**

## 9. Is current preference authority actually too weak? — **No, not on this evidence**

Production was preferred for both athletes. The four phases from A7.20 that treated low churn as a
defect were measuring a real property of the architecture and assuming, without evidence, that it
was a fault. **It is not established as a fault, and this is the first phase that asked.**

Two qualifications, stated because they bound the finding rather than soften it:

1. **Two athletes, one universe, one evaluator.** Men's soccer only, and the evaluator is not
   independent of the generator.
2. **This does not prove production's lists are good.** §12 shows they are not. It shows that the
   proposed cure is worse than the disease, and that the disease was misdiagnosed.

## 10. Does stronger authority improve recommendation quality? — **No**

Both stronger variants were judged worse on both athletes, and the preference signal moved in the
correct direction in every case. **The failure is not that the term does not work. It is that the
component it amplifies is confounded** — with programme weakness for `playingPathway`, with cost and
with admissions selectivity for `academicStrengthFit`. Amplifying a confounded signal amplifies the
confound.

## 11. Where does stronger authority go too far? — **Immediately**

τ = 0.40 already: V3-C median strength 56.5 → 54.7 and below-level 31 → 47; V3-E over-budget
83 → 93 and median net price up $1,595. Both moderate lists were judged worse than production.
**There is no safe τ in this family; the damage starts at the smallest value tested.**

**A measurement error of mine, recorded.** Blind, I marked V3-C's Lists A and B *"TOO
CONSERVATIVE"* — and they were the τ = 0.70 and τ = 0.40 variants, i.e. the ones with **more**
playing authority. The verdict on quality was right and the attribution of cause was wrong: I read
"prestige at the top" from Chicago and Amherst leading List A, when in fact the term promoted them
because a squad with five of eight forwards departing scores high on `playingPathway`. **The
too-conservative/too-aggressive answer should therefore not be read as evidence about authority
level on V3-C.** Everything in §7 is a direct measurement and stands independently.

## 12. What preference behaviour the evidence supports

1. **Preference as a weight on evidence that is already sound.** Where the evidence is confounded,
   more authority is more confound. Fix the evidence before the authority.
2. **Academic preference needs an admissibility counterpart.** The model already disciplines
   athletic reach — Candidate G's level anchor exists for exactly this — and applies **nothing** of
   the kind to academic reach. Johns Hopkins (6%), Chicago (5%), Caltech (3%) and Williams (8%)
   appear in all three V3-E lists against a 3.4 GPA and a 1150 SAT. **This is the largest defect
   this phase found and it is present in production.**
3. **The declared major should behave as an academic requirement, not a component.** Seven or eight
   programmes per list do not teach Engineering and are ranked above those that do, on rating alone.
   `majorFit` 1.20 is doing its job inside Opportunity; the ordering failure is that institutional
   rating is allowed to outweigh the athlete's one concrete academic statement.
4. **`playingPathway` should be read against programme quality, not in isolation.** An empty forward
   room at a 34.0-strength side is not the same opportunity as an empty forward room at a 60.0-strength
   side, and the component cannot currently tell them apart.

## 13. Is an architecture repair justified? — **Not the one four phases have been designing**

The preference-authority repair is **not** justified. Every candidate in this family — A7.24's
`C_authority`, both τ values here, and P4 before them — is now either rejected on semantics or
rejected on recruitment judgement.

Two *different* repairs are indicated by this phase's evidence, both in the evidence layer rather
than the authority layer, and neither is preregistered or tested:

- **an academic-reach discipline**, the counterpart to the athletic level anchor;
- **major-as-requirement ordering**, so a programme that does not teach the declared degree cannot
  be promoted over one that does on institutional rating alone.

## 14. Smallest next experiment

**Measure the academic-reach gap before designing anything for it.** One read-only diagnostic over
the existing V3/W3 profiles, no scoring change:

1. For every programme in each athlete's top 100, the gap between the athlete's SAT/GPA and the
   institution's median, and the admit rate.
2. How many top-100 places go to programmes where that gap exceeds what athletic recruitment
   plausibly bridges.
3. The same measurement at academic priorities 1, 3 and 5, to establish whether the preference
   creates the exposure or merely reveals it.
4. The same for the declared major: how often a non-offering programme outranks an offering one,
   and by how much.

That is one harness run and it decides whether either repair in §13 is worth a tournament. **No
new labelling, no new athletes, no scoring change.**

## 15. Unresolved operator decisions

1. **Is the academic-reach gap a defect to fix, or correct behaviour?** A recruited athlete does get
   admissions latitude. Nobody has decided how much, and the model currently assumes unlimited.
   **Until this is decided, §13's first repair cannot be specified.**
2. **Should a declared major be able to outrank institutional rating?** A7.21 locked "direct fit
   information, not academic prestige and not a filter". This phase shows rating routinely beating
   it in the ordering. Whether that is the locked semantics failing or the locked semantics working
   as intended is a product call.
3. **τ(3).** A7.21 froze τ(3) = 0; A7.24's `C_authority` used 0.20. Both athletes here state their
   focal preference at 5, so **this phase does not settle it** and it remains open.
4. **Is the preference–Financial tension acceptable?** Carried unchanged from A7.24 and now
   measured on a second surface: academic authority removes the affordable half of the list because
   cheap programmes have weaker academics.
