# A7.25 — blind recruitment validation of preference authority

**Written and committed BEFORE any variant output was generated or inspected.** Nothing in this
phase changes production. Baseline is `0ae0399` (Candidate G + `majorFit` 1.20). P4 is withdrawn
and does not appear.

---

## 1. The question this phase exists to answer

A7.20–A7.24 all proceeded from an assumption that has never been tested against recruitment
judgement: that playing-opportunity and academic-strength preference authority is **too weak**.
Every tournament since A7.20 has treated raising it as the goal and argued only about how.

A7.24 closed with the recommendation that this assumption be tested before any further
architecture work. This phase tests it.

**Top-100 churn is not the objective.** It is reported after unblinding, for context only. The
25–35 region named in A7.21 was a diagnostic intuition and is not ground truth here.

## 2. Athletes — frozen, not created

The two existing V3 profiles whose stated preference is the one under test. Neither is modified.

| id | ability | position | origin | contribution | level | playing | academic | major |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V3-C** strong / playing-focused | 7 | Forward | International (England) | $18,000 | 2 | **5** | 3 | none |
| **V3-E** academic-priority | 6 | Defender | Austin, TX | $13,000 | 3 | 3 | **5** | Engineering |

## 3. Variants — three per athlete, frozen here

All three run the **identical production pipeline**: same layers, same weights, same gates, same
calibration, same `majorFit` 1.20, same Candidate G. They differ by exactly one term, applied to
Pursuit **after** the gates and **before** the sort, exactly where A7.20 established that
preference authority actually lives.

```
authority(v, τ) = 1 − τ · (1 − v)                     v = the focal programme-side signal, on [0,1]
P' = P × authority(v, τ)
```

**Non-inverting by construction.** `authority ≤ 1` for every `v`, so a weak signal is never
rewarded and a strong one is never penalised. At `v = 1` the factor is exactly 1. This is the
`C_authority` form from A7.24, the only design tested there that satisfies the locked semantics.

| label | τ | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **control** | — | production as shipped, no additional term |
| **moderate** | **0.40** | A7.24's `C_authority` τ(5) |
| **strong** | **0.70** | stress point; the largest bounded penalty short of a filter |

**Focal signal, one per athlete, fixed:**

- **V3-C** → `playingPathway`, the Opportunity component value.
- **V3-E** → `academicStrengthFit`, the Opportunity component value. At academic priority 5 this
  equals the raw academic percentile, so the term acts on preference-free evidence.

**The term is applied only to the athlete's own stated-5 preference.** No other preference is
touched in any variant, so the experiment isolates the one axis under test. Because both athletes
state their focal preference at 5, the τ(3) question A7.21 and A7.24 disagreed on does not arise
here and is NOT settled by this phase.

**When the focal component is absent or unscoreable, the factor is exactly 1.** No evidence means
no adjustment; it must never mean a penalty.

`majorFit` is 1.20 in all three variants and is not swept. Financial formulas, gates,
Recruitability and the level anchor are untouched in all three.

## 4. Blinding protocol, and its honest limit

1. The variant → display-letter mapping is computed inside the generator from a derived hash (no
   RNG), written to `A7.25-*.sealed.json` **and nowhere else**, and not printed.
2. The blind pack carries: programme facts from the audited `programmeFacts` whitelist, and three
   ordered lists referencing programmes by a blind code. It carries **no** score, **no** Pursuit
   value, **no** layer value, **no** variant identity, **no** indication of which list is
   production, and **no** churn figure.
3. Blind codes are assigned by hashing and sorting, so a code cannot track rank in any variant.
4. Judgements are written to `A7.25-*.review.json` and **git-committed** before the sealed file is
   opened. Commit order is the proof.

**THE LIMIT, STATED PLAINLY.** The generator and the evaluator are the same agent. This is not the
blindness of an independent human reviewer and is not presented as one. What the protocol does
guarantee is that no judgement was written with the variant mapping visible, and that the mapping
was fixed before any list was read. What it cannot guarantee is that a list leaning visibly harder
on playing pathway was not *inferred* to be a non-control. That inference is not treated as
leakage: whether a list leans too hard on the stated preference is precisely what the evaluator is
being asked, and answering it is the task.

## 5. What the evaluator is asked — and what it is NOT asked

The evaluator judges **programme recommendations**, never architectures.

For each list, seven outputs:

1. programmes belonging in the first wave
2. reasonable but lower priority
3. questionable
4. should probably be substantially lower
5. important programme types missing from the first wave
6. whether the list is too conservative or too aggressive on the athlete's stated preference
7. specific trade-offs believed to be wrong

**The evaluator is NOT told**: which variant is production, that current authority was suspected
weak, any expected or observed churn figure, or any numeric algorithm score. **No numeric score of
any list is requested.**

V3-C is judged on level, recruitability, playing pathway, financial viability and other fit
evidence. V3-E is judged on level, recruitability, academic strength, declared-major fit,
financial viability and other fit evidence.

## 6. Pairwise disagreements — selection rule, frozen

After the independent reviews are written, matched disagreements are presented without their
source. A disagreement is a programme whose rank differs between two variants by **≥ 20 places**,
or which appears in one list's first 25 and not in another's.

Selection is **mechanical**, by the dimension on which the two programmes differ most, so that the
set spans: playing pathway, academic strength, financial viability, competitive level, major fit.
No programme is selected by name or by whether its placement looks right.

## 7. The Financial trade-off — required, answer not encoded

For V3-E the disagreement set must explicitly include cases where **stronger academic authority**
and **better affordability** conflict — a more academically capable but harder-to-afford programme
placed above a cheaper alternative. A7.24 measured `corr(academic rating, F) = −0.270`, so these
cases exist structurally and are not contrived.

The question put to the evaluator is open: *for an academically focused athlete, when should
academic quality justify ranking a financially harder programme above a cheaper alternative?* No
preferred answer is stated anywhere in the pack.

For V3-C the equivalent conflicts are required: better playing pathway against better
affordability, against better recruitability, and against higher competitive level.

## 8. Analysis after unblinding

Reported: which architecture produced each list; where judgement preferred the control; where it
preferred stronger authority; whether responsiveness improved recommendation quality; whether
stronger authority damaged Financial or recruitability behaviour; and whether the evidence points
at current, moderate or stronger authority.

**No single winner is declared.** The deliverable is the trade-off surface.

**If the evaluation does not clearly support stronger authority, production is preserved and the
phase says so.**

## 9. Declared in advance

- No scoring file is modified. The variant term lives in a harness script that production does not
  import.
- τ is not tuned after seeing output. 0.40 and 0.70 are fixed by this document.
- No programme is named in any selection rule.
- If the reviews do not discriminate between the lists, the phase reports the design as
  non-discriminating and stops, as A7.17 did.
- **DO NOT IMPLEMENT** applies to the whole phase.
